## Guidelines

### Remember the Process

Your thesis should emerge from your analysis, not motivate it. You've spent P04-P07 building understanding, developing analytical insights, and determining significance. P08 brings all this work together in polished form.

Don't worry whether your thesis reaches the stretch goal or whether a claim counts as an implicit disagreement or not. Those labels describe what careful analysis tends to produce; they aren't targets, and you shouldn't use those phrases in your paper. Instead, focus on the basics: check your understanding of each position against the text, and work out what really drives the disagreement rather than restating it in other words. If you arrive at original insights or interesting implications from that work, whether through ideas we discussed in your conference or on your own, develop them in the body. Trying to reach the stretch goal directly usually weakens the analysis it depends on.

### Revising your draft

Start from the feedback on your draft: the recorded feedback and what we discussed in your conference. Make the main changes we discussed before anything else.

Revise from the big picture down. Work on your thesis and analysis first, then on how your paragraphs and sentences express them, and polish last, since polishing a paragraph you later cut or rewrite wastes your time. The revision lists under Requirements follow that order.

**Keep using your outline.** You don't submit an outline with your final paper, but I recommend keeping the outline from your draft and using it as you revise, going back and forth between the outline and the draft as we discussed in class. [:How to create and use your outline](/course-ntw2029/assignments/papers/p2-p07-full-draft-and-outline-2610/#x-how-to-create-and-use-your-outline)

- When a revision changes what a paragraph is about, update its topic sentence in the outline.
- When a sentence doesn't fit its paragraph's topic sentence, the fix may be in the outline rather than the paragraph: moving the sentence to another paragraph can change that paragraph's topic sentence too.
- If your thesis changed after the conference, check each topic sentence in the outline against the new thesis, and revise or cut the paragraphs that no longer support it.
- Read through the outline from start to finish to check that your argument flows and that each paragraph advances your thesis.
