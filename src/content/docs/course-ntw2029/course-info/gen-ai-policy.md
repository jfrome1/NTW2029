GenAI is a tool we'll all be using more and more, so this course lets you explore its capabilities and limitations without penalty. The key is transparency—I need to see how you're using it to understand what helps or hinders your learning.

## GenAI Policy Requirements

You may use GenAI chatbots for any coursework with no grade penalty if you follow these requirements:

- For each submitted assignment:
	- If you use GenAI, submit links to all relevant chat transcripts
	- If you don't use GenAI, submit the statement "I did not use GenAI in any way for this assignment"
- Submit links/non-use statements at the top of the assignment, above the title
- Copy links using the share function, not browser URLs
- Use only text-based chatbot interactions (prompts and responses)
- Conduct all GenAI conversations in English
- Create a non-shared account for your GenAI platform and stay logged in when using it to enable link sharing
- Don't delete any course chats

Additional essential details below.

## Content Responsibility

You are responsible for all content you submit, regardless of its source. GenAI tools frequently fabricate citations, misrepresent arguments, and invent information that sounds plausible but is false. Assume GenAI is wrong about factual claims.

If you use information from GenAI in your work, you must:

1. Verify the information with a reputable published source
2. Cite that source in your work

Do not cite GenAI as a source for factual claims.

### Grade Impact

- GenAI use or non-use does not affect your grade if you declare it properly
- Assignments missing both GenAI links and non-use statement receive zero credit
- Non-declaration or prohibited uses are considered academic honesty violations, with serious penalties

### Non-Approved GenAI Uses

- Uploading or downloading files to/from GenAI tools
- Using GenAI integrated into other apps, such as Microsoft Office, Google Docs, or custom IDEs
- Creating, editing, and chatting about docs within the AI interface (e.g. the ChatGPT `/canvas` function, NotebookLM, "Deep Research" reports or similar)
- GenAI models that are not confirmed to create special links that allow me to download conversations (ChatGPT, Copilot, and Gemini are approved; Claude and DeepSeek are not approved)

### New Chat Guidelines

- Start a new chat for each assignment to keep chats organized
- Try to use just one chat per assignment (P01-P08 count as separate assignments)
- If you forget and have more than one chat for an assignment, submit links to all of them
- Submit entire chats regardless of length

### What Counts as GenAI Use

Any interaction with GenAI while working on an assignment counts as use, including:

- Checking spelling or grammar
- Asking questions that prove unhelpful
- Testing the tool without using its output
- Getting ideas or feedback

Submit all chats about course topics, whether or not you actually use anything from the chat.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Why only chatbots?

Chatbot interactions provide clear documentation of your thinking process and the AI's contributions. Other GenAI make it difficult to:

- Understand what work is yours versus the AI's
- Track your learning process
- Evaluate the tool's impact on student development

Text-based chat transcripts show the complete interaction, making it possible to study how students use these tools and how they affect learning outcomes.

### Why are certain uses prohibited?

I need transparency to understand how you're using these tools and what you're learning. Chatbot transcripts provide that transparency, while file uploads and integrated tools don't. Also, basic chatbot features are free for all students, but advanced features often require payment. Restricting use to free features keeps things more fair for everyone.

### Why submit unhelpful or dead-end conversations?

It's valuable to know what students try that doesn't work and what AI tools can't do well. Understanding failed attempts helps me teach you better strategies and shows me where AI falls short. These "failed" conversations are often the most instructive for research purposes.

### Research purpose

This permissive policy isn't a trick or test. We genuinely want to understand how students use GenAI tools when there's no penalty for being honest about it.

The research aims to understand:

- How students actually use GenAI when given freedom to do so
- Which uses help or hinder learning
- How to better integrate these tools into education

Your honest use and documentation helps us develop better teaching approaches for future students.

### How does the instructor use GenAI?

I may use GenAI to:

- Generate questions for discussion, assignments, or quizzes
- Create writing examples for class discussion
- Revise course materials and handouts
- Edit (but not write) assignment feedback

The key principle: you must be transparent about any assistance you receive, whether from humans or AI. This course requires that transparency through chat transcript submission.

### Is there really no penalty for using GenAI?

That's right - no grade penalty as long as you submit your chat transcript links. Your learning may suffer if you rely too heavily on it, but there's no policy violation.

### Can I upload files to ChatGPT?

File uploads and downloads are not allowed under course policy. You can copy-paste text into the chat, but no file uploads. This includes features like ChatGPT Canvas.

### What if I forget my GenAI declaration?

Every assignment must include either a GenAI link or statement of non-use to earn credit. Since there's no penalty for use, please don't risk getting a zero by forgetting the disclosure.

For exercises, if the declaration is missing, the assignment will be marked "incomplete" and you can resubmit with a working link within a week to get half credit. If not, no credit.

For papers, DON'T FORGET your declaration and check all links, because lack of declaration is an academic honesty violation.

### Do I have to use GenAI?

No, you're not expected to use GenAI. If you don't use it, just end your submission with: "I did not use GenAI in any way for this assignment."

I use GenAI in my work, and, in this course you can use GenAI as well. Using it is not shameful and will not make me think less of your ability. It can be a useful tool for improving your writing. However, it can also be used in ways that interfere with learning how to write and think.

This course allows you to use GenAI as a conversation partner, but it cannot be used to actually write any of your assignments. While you are free to use ideas, feedback, explanations, or other conversational aspects of GenAI chats, you must be the author of all work you submit, as described below.

My teaching is based on trust and instructor transparency, and my previous GenAI policy relied on an honor system. Unfortunately a small number of students' interest in maximizing grade-to-effort ratio made it impossible for me to continue that policy without being unfair to the remaining students. Thus, I've adopted a system that requires more transparency on the student writing process.

The course GenAI policy has four main requirements, and the rest of this page provides the detail behind them:

1. Use only GenAI tools that provide share links showing me your conversations.

2. Disclose links to all relevant chats, so I can see what you actually did.

4. Be able to explain your work and account for your writing choices.

3. Write in Google Docs, and type your submitted work without pasting text into it (exceptions noted below)

## 1. What you can use

Use chatbots, and only chatbots. The approved ones are Copilot, Gemini, Claude, and ChatGPT. If you want to use a different chatbot, ask me, and don't use it until I've added it here.

The test behind this: whatever you do with GenAI has to happen somewhere that gives you a shareable link to the conversation itself, so I can read what you and the AI actually said.

You may upload material to ask about, but don't download anything, or copy any writing generated by AI. Don't let it edit or create files. Just chat with it in the chat interface. (A few exceptions are listed under "How text gets into your document".)

So these aren't allowed:

- GenAI that gives you no link to the conversation, such as NotebookLM, the AI built into PDF readers like Adobe, or the writing assistance built into Microsoft Word and Google Docs. Some of these have a share button, but what it shares is your documents, so whoever you send it to can chat with them too. It doesn't show me your conversation.
- GenAI features that write to documents, such as the ChatGPT `/canvas` function or AI-generated research reports.

Note that the version history Google Docs keeps is not GenAI, and this rule doesn't affect it.

## 2. How you disclose GenAI use and share your writing process

- If you used GenAI, submit links to all of the chats you had while working on the assignment. [:how to create a share link](#x-how-to-create-a-share-link)
- If you didn't use GenAI, submit the statement "I did not use GenAI in any way for this assignment."
- Put your links or your non-use statement at the top of the assignment, above the title.
- Start a new chat for each assignment. [:how to organize your chats](#x-how-to-organize-your-chats)
- Write your assignments in Google Docs, share each document with me, and submit its link with the assignment. [:how to share your documents](#x-how-to-share-your-documents)
- Copy links using the share function, not the browser URL.

:::danger Browser URLs do not work. You must use the share function to create public links. :::

### What submitting your links says

Submitting your links says that this is all of your GenAI use for the assignment. Submitting one link says that chat was your only GenAI exchange for it. Submitting the non-use statement says you used none. [:why I ask for links](#x-why-i-ask-for-links)

A chat you leave out isn't a paperwork slip. Leaving one out says you used GenAI in a way you didn't, which is a false statement about your own work.

Your declaration is complete when you've handed over what you were asked to hand over. There's no separate judgment about whether you described your use accurately.

Submitting links to unusual, seemingly manufactured chats suggests lack of honesty in disclosure.

### What counts as GenAI use

Any interaction with GenAI while working on an assignment counts as use, including:

- Checking spelling or grammar
- Asking questions that prove unhelpful
- Trying the tool without using its output
- Getting ideas or feedback
- Asking GenAI to "grade" your work

Submit all chats about course topics, whether or not you actually use anything from the chat.

If you ask anyone for feedback on an assignment, a classmate, a tutor, a friend, or family, their GenAI use on your work has to follow the same rules yours does, and it's on you to tell them. They must use an approved chatbot and send you the chat link, and you disclose it just as if you had used GenAI yourself.

## 3. How text gets into your document

Type everything in your document yourself. Don't paste in text from other locations, whether or not you wrote it yourself, apart from the exceptions listed below.

Each paper stays in one document the whole way through, rather than getting a new one at each stage. Paper 1's document carries P01, if you do it, then P02 and P03. Paper 2's document starts at P06 and carries P07, P08, and P09. Every other assignment gets a document of its own.

You can copy and paste in the following things, none of which are your own prose:

- Quotations, with their citations
- The assignment question or prompt you're answering
- Reference list entries and citation strings
- Your chat share links
- Your Google Docs link
- Your declaration or non-use statement

You can ask a chatbot anything you want. What you can't do is put its words in your draft. If you want a chatbot's help with your writing, paste your writing into the chat, get the feedback, and type the changes into your document yourself.

## 4. Being responsible for your work, and how I check

You are responsible for all content you submit, regardless of its source. Taking responsibility for the work means that you submit work that you wrote and that you have intellectual ownership of the work.

Being responsible isn't limited to answering for errors. It also means claiming what you submit as your own. You get credit for work you have intellectual ownership of, and work you can't account for doesn't earn credit. That isn't a penalty for dishonesty. It's what earning credit for your own work means.

So your work has to be yours in two ways. [:why I ask about your choices](#x-why-i-ask-about-your-choices)

- You can explain what's in your paper in a way that makes sense, and you can answer questions and objections about it.
- You can account for how you wrote it: the choices you made, the other options you considered, and why you chose as you did, in terms of the writing concepts we cover in class.

This standard applies to a paper and to every assignment leading up to it, not only to the version you submit at the end.

You'll discuss your paper with me in scheduled conferences during the term. Those discussions are one of several inputs I consider in determining a grade for the paper, alongside everything else I see. I'll give you specific information later about my grading criteria, including this one.

## Content responsibility

GenAI tools frequently fabricate citations, misrepresent arguments, and invent information that sounds plausible but is false. Just as you would check a smart friend's claims before putting them in an assignment, you should do the same for GenAI's claims. Verify claims with a reputable published source, and cite that source in your work.

Being responsible is not limited to being penalized for errors. It also means that you only get credit for work that you are responsible for in the sense of having intellectual ownership of the work and writing the words submitted. If you submit work that you do not fully understand, cannot explain, or cannot provide a reasonable account of how it was created, you won't receive credit for it.

## Using GenAI well

The uses I encourage are the ones that leave you able to meet the standard above, and the uses I'd steer you away from are the ones that leave you unable to. Some suggestions:

- Ask a chatbot to explain something you didn't understand in a reading, then check the explanation against the reading itself.
- Give it your idea and ask what's wrong with it. Arguing with a chatbot about your own thinking is one of the things it's genuinely good at.
- Ask it to find the problems in your draft and tell you what they are, then make the corrections yourself. Asking it to fix your draft and taking the fixed text back gets you a better paragraph and no better at writing.
- Try not to hand over a decision you'd have to explain later. If you can't say why a claim is in your paper, or why your paragraphs are in the order they're in, you probably didn't make that choice.

I'm not worried about how much you use GenAI, I'm encouraging you to be the actual author of your work.

## Your record-keeping responsibilities

Your links are the record of your GenAI use and writing process, and the record has to stay readable for the whole semester. Thus, you are responsible for keeping them available and organized:

- Use your own GenAI account, and stay logged in when you're working, so your share links work.
- Keep every chat you use for this course until the semester ends. Don't delete any of them.
- If you're using an account someone else has access to, the links are still your responsibility. A link that stops working because someone else deleted the chat behind it is a link you can't produce, which raises suspicion.

## How this policy can affect your grade

- It's fine to use GenAI, and it's fine not to. Using it doesn't give you less credit for your work, and it doesn't make me think less of you. What matters is how you use it, and whether you declare it in full.
- Failure to follow any of these rules, including incomplete or inaccurate disclosure, can lead to a penalty up to full loss of credit (a zero) on the relevant assignment.
- If the incomplete or inaccurate disclosure constitutes not just a course policy violation, but plagiarism or academic misconduct, more serious penalties are possible, including failing the course.

## Academic honesty

NUS defines plagiarism as presenting the work or ideas of another person or entity, including AI, as your own without proper attribution. Declaring your GenAI use is that attribution.

So declaring matters twice over. Declare, and your use isn't plagiarism, whatever you used GenAI for. Don't declare, and your use is plagiarism, under the university's definition rather than under a rule I invented, and I have to treat the matter as an academic honesty issue rather than as a course penalty.

That's also the difference between the two ways an unapproved tool can go. Tell me you used one and it stays a course matter. Conceal it and it's a missing declaration, which puts it here instead.

Supplying a missing declaration later doesn't change that. Making an assignment complete fixes your grade for it; the academic honesty question is separate.

## What I collect, and what I do with it

Work you do for this course is visible to me. I collect links to your course chats, a record of how your documents were written, and our conversations about your papers.

The document record comes from a Google Docs add-on called Process Feedback. It reads the version history Google already keeps and turns it into a playback of how the writing developed. You don't install anything or do anything differently, and there's nothing in it I can see that you can't. You can read about it at processfeedback.org.

To protect your privacy, keep a separate area for this course's chats so nothing unrelated to the course ends up in a link you submit.

I do read the links. Not every one of them, but any of them, whenever it seems useful or I'm curious.

##### :x why I ask for links

When people describe their GenAI use in a sentence, the vaguer the description, the more of the work they get to claim, and a vague description is still a description. Links don't work that way. A chat can't say less than what's in it, so the record does the describing for you, and you never have to judge how much detail is enough.

##### :x why I ask about your choices

I'm not trying to work out how much of your paper you typed. What I care about is whether you can do the thinking and writing this course teaches, and the way I find that out is by asking you about your own paper. A student who used no GenAI and can't explain their argument is in exactly the same position as one who used a lot and can't.
