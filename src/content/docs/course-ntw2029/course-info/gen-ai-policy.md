## AI Policy summary

- You should use AI only as a conversation partner about your work. Therefore: use only chatbots, and only approved chatbots.
- You should do all writing associated with your assignment, and AI should do none of the writing. Therefore: write all of your assignments in Google Docs, and don't copy/paste anything into the assignment (with some exceptions).
- You should take responsibility for all work you submit. Therefore: submit work that reflects your choices, and be ready to account for those choices.

This page contains important details below that you must be familiar with in order to follow course policy and earn credit for the course. Don't rely on the summary above.

## Why this policy exists

My AI policy is driven by two main goals:

- Students should learn as much as possible in the course
- Grading should be fair, and students who violate course policy should never gain advantage over honest students

Both of these goals rely on my ability to evaluate your writing and thinking skills. I can't teach you effectively if I don't know what you can and can't do, and I can't grade fairly if assignments don't reflect your abilities.

AI has made it much harder to evaluate a student's writing and thinking abilities on the basis of the assignments they submit alone.

Further, AI can also be used in ways that interfere with learning how to write and think. You can't learn to organize a paper if the AI makes the choices about how to organize. Thus, so the course allows some uses and prohibits others.

My policy is not anti-AI as a whole. I use GenAI in my work, and in this course, you can use GenAI as well. AI can be a useful tool for improving your writing. Using it is not shameful and will not make me think less of your ability. I just want you to use it in a way that allows us to achieve these main course goals of instruction and fairness.

## You should use AI only as a conversation partner about your work.

To learn how to write and think, you must do the writing and thinking. AI can be a great tool for talking about writing and ideas, but you must actually wrestle with the relevant choices to build your skills.

## You should do all writing associated with your assignment, and AI should do none of the writing.

Doing the writing is the only way to actually learn how to write. You can't learn how to write by guiding an AI to write something; you build the skill by producing the sentences yourself.

When you start an assignment:

- Write each assignment in its own new Google Doc, and share the document with me. [How to share a Google Doc](https://processfeedback.org/docs/how-to-provide-edit-access-in-gdocs/).
- When your assignment continues work from a previous assignment (for example, your Paper 1 final continues your draft), start the new assignment by pasting your entire previous submission into a new document.

While you write:

- Type everything in your document yourself. Don't paste in text from anywhere else, whether or not you wrote it yourself, except for these things: [:why can't I paste in my own writing?](#x-why-cant-i-paste-in-my-own-writing)
	- Pasting the previous submission into a new document to start the next assignment
	- Quotations, with their citations
	- The assignment question or prompt you're answering
	- Reference list entries and citation strings
	- Your chat share links
	- Your Google Docs link
	- Your declaration or non-use statement
- Moving text around within your Google Doc is fine: you can cut text from one part of the document and paste it elsewhere in the same document.

When you submit an assignment:

- Submit your Google Doc's link at the top of the assignment, with your name and other header information: below any GenAI links or non-use statement, and above the title.

At your conferences, I'll ask you questions about how you wrote your paper and the choices you made, to help me evaluate what you've learned about the writing process.

How well your writing choices reflect the guidance taught in the course is one factor in how I grade your paper.

## You should take responsibility for all work you submit.

You are responsible for all content you submit, regardless of its source. Taking responsibility means two things:

- You have intellectual ownership of your work: you understand what your work means.
- You answer for your work's errors, whatever their source.

Intellectual ownership is demonstrated not through the paper itself, but through our interactions about your paper, which happen in class, in our scheduled conferences, and informally outside of class. Your ownership becomes obvious as I ask you questions about your source, what it says, and how you're thinking about it.

The expectation is that you can explain what's in your paper in a way that makes sense, and that you can answer questions and objections about it. You only earn credit for work you understand and intellectually own. That isn't a penalty for dishonesty. It's what earning credit for your own work means.

This standard applies to a paper and to every assignment leading up to it, not only to the version you submit at the end. Our conversations are one of several inputs I consider in determining a grade for the paper, alongside everything else I see.

Answering for errors doesn't change when they come from GenAI. Don't think of GenAI as a source: a source is a published piece of work that others can see and check, while a chatbot is literally a conversation partner, similar to a friend you might talk with about your paper. GenAI tools frequently fabricate citations, misrepresent arguments, and invent information that sounds plausible but is false. Just as you would check a smart friend's claims before putting them in an assignment, check GenAI's claims: verify them with a reputable published source, and cite that source, not the chatbot, in your work.

At your conferences, come prepared to discuss and answer questions about your paper.

## What to submit with every assignment

You must share all relevant chat links and Google Docs links for every assignment, and keep all of your chats and documents, as evidence that you've followed the course policies:

At the top of every assignment, above the title:

- The link to the Google Doc you wrote the assignment in. [:how to share your documents](#x-how-to-share-your-documents)
- Links to all of the chats you had while working on it, or, if you used no GenAI, the statement "I did not use GenAI in any way for this assignment." [:how to create an AI chat shared link](#x-how-to-create-an-ai-chat-shared-link)

Getting the links right:

- Copy links using the share function, not the browser URL. Browser URLs do not work for anyone else.
- Start a new chat for each assignment (P01 through P09 count as separate assignments). If you end up with more than one chat for an assignment, submit links to all of them.

### What submitting your links says

Submitting your links says that this is all of your GenAI use for the assignment. Submitting one link says that chat was your only GenAI exchange for it. Submitting the non-use statement says you used none. [:why I ask for links](#x-why-i-ask-for-links)

A chat you leave out isn't a paperwork slip. Leaving one out says you used GenAI in a way you didn't, which is a false statement about your own work.

I have now seen hundreds of student AI chats, and it's fairly obvious which are real and which have been manufactured for the purpose of submitting to an instructor. Submitting links to unusual chats suggests lack of honesty in disclosure.

### What I do with your records

The document record comes from a Google Docs add-on called Process Feedback. It reads the version history Google already keeps and turns it into a playback of how the writing developed. You don't install anything or do anything differently, and there's nothing in it I can see that you can't. You can read about it at processfeedback.org.

I do click most of the submitted chat links, and I read a good number in detail.

## How this policy can affect your grade

- It's fine to use GenAI, and it's fine not to. Using it doesn't give you less credit for your work, and it doesn't make me think less of you. What matters is whether you use it within course policies and whether you declare it in full.
- Failure to follow any of these rules, including incomplete or inaccurate disclosure, can lead to a penalty up to full loss of credit (a zero) on the relevant assignment.
- If the incomplete or inaccurate disclosure constitutes not just a course policy violation, but plagiarism or academic misconduct, more serious penalties are possible, including failing the course.

## Academic honesty

NUS defines plagiarism as presenting the work or ideas of another person or entity, including AI, as your own without proper attribution. Declaring your GenAI use is that attribution.

So declaring matters twice over. Declare, and your use isn't plagiarism, whatever you used GenAI for. Don't declare, and your use is plagiarism, under the university's definition rather than under a rule I invented, and I have to treat the matter as an academic honesty issue rather than as a course penalty.

Supplying a missing declaration later doesn't change that. Making an assignment complete fixes your grade for it; the academic honesty question is separate.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Why only chatbots?

A chatbot conversation gives me a link I can open and read. A tool that produces no shareable transcript gives me nothing, so I can't tell what work is yours and what the tool did, and I can't see your learning process. Also, basic chatbot features are free for all students, while advanced features often require payment, so restricting use to free features keeps things more fair for everyone.

### Why can't I draft in Word and paste it in?

Because that would make it much harder to tell whether you or AI wrote your work. It's annoying/unfortunate, but that's our reality.

### Why submit unhelpful or dead-end conversations?

It's valuable to know what students try that doesn't work and what AI tools can't do well. Understanding failed attempts helps me teach you better strategies and shows me where AI falls short. These "failed" conversations are often the most instructive for research purposes.

### Do you use the chats for anything outside of class?

Yes, I am studying how students use GenAI, and I analyze anonymized chat transcripts and assignment submissions for research after grades are submitted. Participation is optional. Although you must follow this GenAI policy, you may opt-out of being part of that research by emailing me at any time up to 2 weeks after final grades are released.

The research aims to understand:

- How students actually use GenAI when given freedom to do so
- Which uses help or hinder learning
- How to better integrate these tools into education

### How does the instructor use GenAI?

I may use GenAI to:

- Generate questions for discussion, assignments, or quizzes
- Create writing examples for class discussion
- Revise course materials and handouts
- Edit (but not write) assignment feedback

### What is the NUS GenAI guidance?

This policy operates within NUS Academic Honesty guidelines.

NUS provides guidance on:

- [General use of AI tools](https://libguides.nus.edu.sg/new2nus/acadintegrity#s-lib-ctab-22144949-4)
- [Using AI tools for assignments](https://libguides.nus.edu.sg/new2nus/acadintegrity#s-lib-ctab-22144949-5)
- [Understanding plagiarism](https://libguides.nus.edu.sg/copyright_essentials_teaching_learning_research/plagiarism)
- [NUS Code of Student Conduct](https://www.nus.edu.sg/celc/statements-and-e-resources-on-plagiarism/)

The key principle: you must be transparent about any assistance you receive, whether from humans or AI. This course requires that transparency through chat transcript submission.

### Can I upload files to a chatbot?

Yes. Upload material to one of the approved chatbots and ask about it. What you can't use are tools that give you no link to your conversation, like NotebookLM or the AI in a PDF reader. NotebookLM's share button shares your documents rather than your chat, so it doesn't count.

### What if I forget my GenAI declaration?

Every assignment must include either a GenAI link or statement of non-use to earn credit. Since there's no penalty for use, please don't risk getting a zero by forgetting the disclosure.

For exercises and paper prep assignments, if the declaration is missing, the assignment will be marked "incomplete" and you can resubmit with a working link within a week to get half credit. If not, no credit.

For papers, DON'T FORGET your declaration and check all links, because lack of declaration is an academic honesty violation.

### Do I have to use GenAI?

No, you're not expected to use GenAI. If you don't use it, just state: "I did not use GenAI in any way for this assignment."
