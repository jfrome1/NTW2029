The course info pages explain my expectations, policies, and requirements for the course. I've created this page because, in some very rare cases, students have been dogged and creative in using ambiguities of language or lawyerly arguments to try to violate course policies without consequences. This page, which is part of the course policies, spells out the details, so that those cases don't come at the expense of honest students.

## GenAI

- Students are responsible not just for reporting generative AI use but for keeping copies of work and accessible versions of work in development in order to provide evidence of appropriate AI use. Such evidence includes all course-related chat logs. Failure to retain evidence of course-related activities may be interpreted as evidence that AI non-use claims are inaccurate.
- The requirement that AI only be used through a chatbot interface and the prohibition on using AI that writes to documents should be read as follows: Students should not use AI in any way that writes to text files or other document-type files on their computer system. Output from the AI should be received only in the form of text printed on the screen directly and not saved to a file for later observation.
- No GenAI use means no GenAI use either directly or indirectly, either by the student or by anyone the student interacts with about their course assignments. A student who interacts with others about their coursework (such as asking them for feedback on potential submissions) must provide those people with a detailed explanation of the course policy and have them agree not to use GenAI on the student's paper unless they follow the same policy requirements as enrolled students. Students are considered responsible for all GenAI use on their work, even if they did not authorize or request it.
- A condition of earning credit is that students can show both intellectual ownership of the work they submit and that they can explain, with specific examples from their own paper, moments of the writing process that reflect the guidance given in the course about how to write a paper. A student who is unable to demonstrate both of these things will not receive credit for the assignment in question. That zero credit is not a penalty for academic dishonesty; it is a grading criterion that must be met to earn credit through the work, and it applies whether or not any GenAI use is established.
- Paper 1 and Paper 2 are graded holistically, and each is developed across its prep assignments rather than written at the end. A prep assignment is marked separately for completion, counting toward the writing development portion of the final grade, but its content is part of the paper it prepares and is an essential input the instructor uses in grading that paper. A prep assignment is therefore not a low-stakes assignment: failing to complete one at minimum acceptable quality means failing the course, and improper GenAI use at any stage bears on the grade for the paper, whether or not that assignment was marked complete. Plagiarism at any stage is plagiarism of the paper assignment as a whole.
- The instructor may ask students to explain or account for any part of their submitted work at any time, including in a meeting arranged for that purpose outside the scheduled conferences.
- A share link shows the conversation as it existed at the moment the link was created. Students are responsible for creating each link after the chat is finished and for ensuring that the link, when opened by someone else, shows the entire chat that took place. A link that shows only part of the actual chat is considered an incomplete disclosure.
- Submitting a Google Doc link will be treated as a claim that the document behind it is a record of the student writing the work submitted. What a student submits must therefore be the work they wrote in that document. The instructor may compare the two, and a submission that differs from the shared document is considered an incomplete disclosure.
- Some assignments may be resubmitted after being marked incomplete, and a missing or inaccurate GenAI declaration may result in a mark of "incomplete". Unlike the other ways an assignment can be incomplete, however, an inaccurate declaration is plagiarism. Resubmitting an assignment with an accurate declaration may let the assignment earn partial credit, but it does not undo the academic honesty violation.

## Submitting work and late penalties

- The late assignment policy summarized as "up to one letter grade per day late" should be read as follows: "up to one letter grade penalty applied at 1 min late and an additional grade penalty of up to one letter grade applied after each additional 24-hour day."

## Penalties are preliminary and not finalized until final grades are submitted

- Any decisions about consequences for course policy violations or for failing to meet assignment standards (including grade penalty determinations) should be considered preliminary until the end of the term. Consequences may be reassessed, even after communicating them to the student, up to the maximum allowable under the course policy, based on a holistic assessment of relevant circumstances.

## Collection of course materials

The instructor collects the following information and/or records the following events during the course:

- all conversations with students, including conferences
- all class meetings
- student AI chats
- student writing in Google Docs
- submitted work
- emails and other messages

The instructor may use collected written material, including transcripts of recordings, in the following ways:

- for improvement of the course
- as inputs for assignment assessment
- as a record of interactions between the instructor and students
- in future term course materials (with names and identifying information removed)
- as evidence in cases of potential academic dishonesty or policy violations

A student may opt out of the above uses with a written request made any time before final grades are released. Due to the difficulty of removing anonymized student information from data sets, the instructor can only guarantee honoring such requests if they occur before final grades are released.

## Other

- Any disagreement with any course policies should be raised proactively before failure to meet those policies. Continued enrollment in the course shall be considered agreement to both the letter and spirit of such policies. Students are voluntarily accepting the stated consequences if they choose not to comply with the policies.
